## WORLD

* **Political Transition in Hungary:** Péter Magyar, the opposition figure who effectively ended Viktor Orbán’s 16-year hold on power, is now actively negotiating a transition of authority. The development has drawn international attention, with President Donald Trump labeling the incoming Prime Minister "a good man," signaling a potential recalibration of U.S.-Hungarian diplomatic relations.
* **Escalation and Diplomacy in the Middle East:** Despite ongoing skirmishes in southern Lebanon between Israeli forces and Hezbollah militants, diplomatic efforts in Washington have intensified. Representatives from Israel and Lebanon are engaged in peace talks, though the stability of these negotiations remains precarious, threatened by broader regional tensions and the fragility of the existing U.S.-Iran cease-fire.

## POLAND

* **State Tribunal Proceedings:** The Sejm has officially received a motion to bring former Minister Zbigniew Ziobro before the State Tribunal. The application, consisting of 250 pages and citing 117 pieces of evidence, represents a significant escalation in the government’s efforts to hold previous administration officials accountable. However, critics argue that the timing—less than 18 months before the next general election—risks appearing as a performative political maneuver rather than an effective pursuit of justice.
* **Administrative Rulings on Security Clearances:** The Supreme Administrative Court (NSA) has dismissed appeals filed by the Prime Minister’s Chancellery regarding the security clearances of Sławomir Cenckiewicz, head of the BBN. While the court’s decision affirms earlier rulings that overturned the revocation of his clearance, the government maintains that this does not automatically grant Cenckiewicz access to classified information, highlighting a continued standoff between the current cabinet and holdovers from the previous administration.
* **Macroeconomic Pressures:** Poland’s economic landscape faces new challenges, as the Central Statistical Office (GUS) reported an annual inflation rate of 3 percent for March. Finance Minister Andrzej Domański has linked the rise directly to global energy price hikes caused by instability in the Middle East, underscoring the susceptibility of the Polish economy to international conflicts.
