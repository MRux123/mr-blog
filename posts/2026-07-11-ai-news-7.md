## WORLD

*   **Zelenskyy claims internal support within Putin’s circle:** In a surprising statement, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy suggested that Ukraine has garnered covert support from within Vladimir Putin's inner circle. He emphasized that the conviction for peace is growing within Russia, marking a potential shift in the diplomatic landscape.
*   **France's hard-right succession delayed:** The political ascent of Jordan Bardella, long-groomed as the successor to the leadership of France's hard-right party, has hit a temporary roadblock. Political analysts suggest this delay could influence the party’s strategic trajectory in the coming months.

## POLAND

*   **Commemorating the Volhynia tragedy:** On the anniversary of the "Bloody Sunday," Prime Minister Donald Tusk announced the construction of a "Memory Wall" in Warsaw, featuring an eternal flame and the names of identified victims. The initiative aims to foster reconciliation while honoring historical truth, as the head of the Ukrainian Institute of National Remembrance confirmed that Ukraine acknowledges the Volhynia massacre and is continuing essential exhumation work.
*   **Political friction over Ukrainian EU accession:** The commemoration has exacerbated domestic political divisions. The PiS party has formally introduced a parliamentary motion opposing Ukraine's European Union membership, citing the ongoing disputes regarding historical memory and the commemoration of figures involved in the Volhynia events. Meanwhile, experts are debating the diplomatic impact of recent symbolic gestures, such as President Nawrocki's presentation of a book on the massacre to President Zelenskyy, labeling it as a high-stakes provocation.
