## WORLD

*   **Escalating Conflict in the Middle East Impacts Global Markets:** The ongoing war between the US/Israel and Iran has reached a critical juncture, with recent strikes on Persian Gulf energy facilities driving a surge in global oil and gas prices. As world leaders gather in Brussels, the fallout from the conflict has overshadowed the original EU economic agenda. Amidst the chaos, the US administration is reportedly considering a new strategy to counter Iranian influence, including the potential release of Iranian crude oil currently held on tankers to stabilize the markets.
*   **International Coalition Mobilizes for Hormuz Security:** In a significant display of military and strategic alignment, six nations—France, the UK, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, and Japan—have formally announced their readiness to collaborate in securing the Strait of Hormuz to ensure the safety of vital maritime energy corridors.
*   **Rising Diplomatic Tensions within Western Alliances:** Pentagon Chief Pete Hegseth has publicly criticized European allies, labeling them "ungrateful" and asserting that they owe President Donald Trump a debt of gratitude for the neutralization of threats posed by Iran. This rhetoric underscores a deepening rift regarding the distribution of security responsibilities and expectations of political deference within NATO.

## POLAND

*   **Orlen Surpasses Gazprom in Market Valuation:** Prime Minister Donald Tusk has touted the recent shift in market capitalization, noting that the Polish energy giant Orlen has now exceeded the valuation of Russia’s Gazprom. Tusk attributed this economic milestone to the government's current management strategies, sparking a sharp retort from opposition leader Mateusz Morawiecki, who accused the Prime Minister of taking credit for established successes.
*   **Anticipated Cabinet Reshuffle:** Reports from government sources indicate that Prime Minister Donald Tusk is preparing a limited cabinet reshuffle. Official confirmations are pending, but current intelligence suggests that the positions of three cabinet ministers are under review, potentially signaling a strategic realignment of the administration.
