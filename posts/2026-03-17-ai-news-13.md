## WORLD

*   **Heightened Tensions in the Middle East:** The security landscape has shifted dramatically as the Israeli military announced the death of Ali Larijani, Iran's de facto leader, following an overnight strike. This development marks a significant escalation in the ongoing conflict, threatening regional stability and complicating international security ties, particularly for Gulf nations reassessing their dependence on U.S. guarantees.
*   **Mass Casualties in Pakistani Airstrike:** A major airstrike conducted by Pakistan in Kabul has resulted in a high death toll, with reports confirming at least 100 fatalities. While Pakistani officials claim the target was an ammunition depot, Afghan authorities report the destruction of a drug rehabilitation center, emphasizing that many of the victims are civilians.
*   **Security Crisis in Nigeria:** The city of Maiduguri has been rocked by a series of suspected suicide attacks targeting critical infrastructure, including market areas and a teaching hospital. The coordinated bombings have left at least 23 people dead and over 100 injured, shattering the relative stability the region had experienced in recent years.

## POLAND

*   **Poland’s Stance on Middle East Conflict:** Prime Minister Donald Tusk has officially declared that Poland will not deploy troops to Iran, stating that the conflict does not directly concern Polish national security. This announcement serves to clarify Poland's position amidst international pressure and internal political debate.
*   **Constitutional Court and Presidential Inquiry:** The Constitutional Tribunal has initiated a formal request to President Karol Nawrocki regarding the status and swearing-in of new judges recently elected by the Sejm. The move highlights the ongoing legal and political friction surrounding the staffing of the country’s highest judicial body.
*   **Smolensk Investigation Update:** The National Prosecutor's Office has partially closed its long-running investigation into the "diplomatic betrayal" allegations following the Smolensk air disaster. The decision effectively drops threads concerning alleged clandestine agreements with Russia, citing a lack of legal framework for cooperation at the time.
