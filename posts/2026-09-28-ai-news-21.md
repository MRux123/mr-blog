## WORLD

*   **Geopolitical Friction Over North Korean Prisoners:** Tensions between Seoul and Kyiv have escalated following reports that Ukraine transferred captured North Korean soldiers to South Korea. The move, which Seoul intended to keep confidential, has triggered a diplomatic summons of the Ukrainian envoy, highlighting the complex and sensitive nature of international military involvement in the ongoing conflict.
*   **Surge in Yemen Conflict:** The World Health Organization has reported over 800 fatalities in a renewed wave of violence between Houthi militias and Saudi-backed government forces. The escalation severely compounds the humanitarian catastrophe in the region, threatening to undo fragile stability efforts.
*   **Russian Drone Strike Targets Science Infrastructure:** A Russian jet-powered drone has struck Ukraine’s National Academy of Sciences, resulting in significant damage to a site of critical intellectual and scientific heritage, marking a new phase in the targeting of non-military national institutions.

## POLAND

*   **Escalating Russian Mobilization:** President Volodymyr Zelensky has issued a stern warning regarding Russia’s latest move to expand its military ranks. Citing intelligence, Zelensky noted that Moscow has begun an additional mobilization effort, potentially incorporating foreign recruits, a move that carries profound implications for the frontline security of Poland and the broader Eastern Flank.
*   **Political Accountability in Hungary:** In a rare move for European politics, the Hungarian Prime Minister has voluntarily waived his parliamentary immunity, facilitating the resumption of a legal investigation involving allegations of theft. This development underscores the ongoing political volatility within Central Europe.
*   **Controversial Sentencing Sparks Debate:** A Polish court's decision to hand down a 3.5-year prison sentence to a Belarusian national for sabotage has triggered a heated domestic debate. Prominent media figures have questioned the proportionality of the punishment given the current security landscape, framing the decision as a potential indicator of broader policy incoherence regarding national defense.
