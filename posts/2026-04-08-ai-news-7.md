## WORLD

* **U.S.-Iran Ceasefire Agreement:** A pivotal two-week ceasefire has been reached between the United States and Iran, effectively de-escalating the immediate threat of conflict in the Strait of Hormuz. The agreement, which follows a strict ultimatum from the Trump administration, seeks to normalize maritime traffic, though it notably excludes the ongoing conflict in Lebanon.
* **Economic Impact:** The easing of geopolitical tensions has triggered a significant market response, with crude oil prices plunging by 15%. While prices remain above pre-conflict levels, the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz is viewed as a major relief for global energy security.
* **Geopolitical Posturing:** The deal has drawn sharp reactions from Moscow, with the Russian Foreign Ministry claiming a role in the diplomatic breakthrough while simultaneously criticizing U.S. foreign policy, framing the administration's recent actions as a failure despite the achieved stability.

## POLAND

* **Economic Relief for Drivers:** The Polish government, represented by Finance Minister Andrzej Domański, has confirmed that the global drop in oil prices will translate into lower fuel costs for domestic consumers. Officials cautioned, however, that the transition from lower wholesale costs to pump prices will take several days to materialize.
* **Strategic NATO Appointment:** Minister of National Defense Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz has officially appointed Lieutenant General Piotr Błazeusz as the Polish Military Representative to the NATO and EU Military Committees. This move is regarded as a critical step in reinforcing Poland’s security architecture within the Western alliance.
* **Public Preparedness:** Amidst broader European discussions on crisis resilience, the Polish Ministry of Finance addressed questions regarding domestic cash reserves. While noting recommendations from central banks in neighboring regions, the government continues to monitor the situation to ensure financial stability for Polish citizens.
