## WORLD

* **Political Realignment in Hungary:** In a historic shift for Central Europe, Péter Magyar has been sworn in as the new Prime Minister of Hungary. His victory marks the end of Viktor Orbán’s 16-year tenure, with Magyar signaling a departure from the previous administration’s policies by pledging to serve the people rather than rule over them.
* **Geopolitical Tensions:** During a scaled-back Victory Day parade, Russian President Vladimir Putin used his address to reiterate his criticism of NATO and defend the ongoing military operations in Ukraine. The rhetoric underscores the deepening divide between Moscow and Western security structures.
* **Populist Gains in Australia:** Australia’s right-wing One Nation party has achieved a milestone, securing its first seat in the lower house. The by-election result is being closely monitored by political analysts as a potential indicator of shifting voter sentiments toward populist platforms.

## POLAND

* **Diplomatic Signaling:** In a significant development, reports suggest that President Vladimir Putin has expressed a conditional readiness to engage in direct talks with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, adding a new, albeit uncertain, dimension to the ongoing diplomatic impasse.
* **Public Reaction to Hungarian Transition:** The swearing-in of Péter Magyar has resonated deeply in Poland, with observers noting the symbolic importance of the event. Polish discourse has focused on the public celebrations in Budapest, which many participants characterized as the definitive end of the "Orbán regime," highlighting a regional interest in the democratic shifts occurring within the Visegrád Group context.
