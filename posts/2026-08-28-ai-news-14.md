## WORLD

* **Escalating Disaster in Nepal:** The humanitarian crisis in the Nepal-Tibet border region has deepened, with the number of missing persons nearly doubling to 1,900. Rescue efforts are being severely hampered by the formation of a precarious barrier lake, which experts fear could trigger catastrophic secondary flooding in the coming days.
* **Geopolitical Strains in Iran:** Six months into the ongoing conflict, Iran appears to be demonstrating unexpected resilience despite aggressive economic pressures from the United States. Reports indicate that the current U.S. strategy of economic warfare, rather than military engagement, is failing to shift Tehran’s core strategic goals while significantly worsening the humanitarian and economic situation for the Iranian population.
* **Security Breach at MAK Vienna:** Austrian authorities are conducting a high-profile investigation following a brazen smash-and-grab heist at the Museum of Applied Arts in Vienna. A historic 200-carat platinum necklace, featuring 673 diamonds and once belonging to Egyptian royalty, was stolen during public operating hours.

## POLAND

* **Judicial Action Following UW Tragedy:** A court has reached a significant decision regarding the suspect, Mieszko R., involved in the brutal crime that occurred at the University of Warsaw, marking a pivotal moment in an investigation that has gripped public opinion.
* **Diplomatic Rhetoric on Ukraine:** In a recent statement regarding the potential conclusion of the war, Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov claimed that Moscow had initially intended to resolve the conflict in a "masculine" and "honorable" manner, sparking fresh debate among international observers and Polish diplomatic circles.
* **New Investigation in Public Administration:** An official investigation has been launched concerning the controversial report authored by Barbara Mróz-Gorgoń. The probe seeks to clarify irregularities linked to the document, which has triggered a significant political and administrative stir.
