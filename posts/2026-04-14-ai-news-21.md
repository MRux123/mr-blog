## WORLD

* **Middle East Diplomacy and Conflict:** Despite ongoing combat in southern Lebanon, diplomatic efforts have intensified with Israel and Lebanon holding high-level peace talks in Washington. Concurrently, tensions remain high as the U.S. blockades Iranian ports, a move criticized by China as a destabilizing factor for the regional ceasefire.
* **Political Earthquake in Hungary:** The landscape of Central European politics has shifted following the landslide defeat of Viktor Orbán by his former ally, Péter Magyar. The result marks a significant transition for Hungary, with the international community closely watching whether Magyar will pivot toward a new governing direction.
* **Humanitarian Crisis in Sudan:** As the conflict in Sudan enters its fourth year, the United Nations has issued a stark warning regarding the deteriorating humanitarian situation. Officials have condemned the lack of global political urgency, describing the crisis as the largest of its kind currently unfolding.

## POLAND

* **Internal Power Struggles within PiS:** Tensions are mounting within the Law and Justice (PiS) party following reports of a "secret meeting" at the party's headquarters. Leadership is reportedly considering a ban on the formation of internal associations, a move interpreted by analysts as an attempt to curtail the influence of former Prime Minister Mateusz Morawiecki.
* **Legal Controversies:** The Minister of Justice has initiated official correspondence with the Hungarian government regarding the cases of Zbigniew Ziobro and Marcin Romanowski, signaling a move to intensify legal scrutiny involving these figures.
* **Strategic Realignments with Ukraine:** Concerns are growing in Warsaw and Kyiv regarding the U.S. administration's stance on military support. Pentagon chief Pete Hegseth’s absence from the Ramstein-format meetings suggests a shifting priority, with the U.S. increasingly pressuring European partners to shoulder the burden of Ukrainian defense.
