## WORLD

*   **Heightened Tensions in the Strait of Hormuz:** The Iranian military has announced that it has reimposed "strict control" over the Strait of Hormuz. Tehran stated that this measure will remain in place until the United States ends its blockade of Iranian ports, escalating uncertainty regarding one of the world's most critical maritime corridors. Analysts highlight Iran's "mosquito fleet" of high-speed vessels as a significant tactical challenge to naval stability in the region.
*   **Humanitarian Relief for Sudan:** International donors have pledged over £1 billion at a Berlin conference to address the catastrophic humanitarian crisis in Sudan. Despite the financial commitment exceeding initial targets, the prospect of an immediate ceasefire remains dim as the conflict enters its third year, leaving millions of civilians in desperate need of aid.
*   **Papal Mission in Angola:** Pope Leo XIV has begun an official visit to Angola, focusing on the historical legacy of colonialism. The trip includes a solemn visit to a site historically linked to the Atlantic slave trade, marking a significant moment in the Vatican's ongoing efforts to address the church's role in colonial history.

## POLAND

*   **Drone Strikes on Russian Infrastructure:** Ukrainian forces have successfully targeted a Russian oil refinery in the Samara region and a port in Vysotsk on the Baltic Sea. These coordinated drone attacks signal a continuing strategic effort by Kyiv to disrupt Russia's energy export capabilities and logistics chains.
*   **Economic Policy Criticism:** The implementation of the National e-Invoicing System (KSeF) has faced sharp public criticism from a former Prime Minister, who condemned the handling of the project as a failure of administrative efficiency. The controversy underscores broader political debates regarding the current government's management of critical economic infrastructure.
