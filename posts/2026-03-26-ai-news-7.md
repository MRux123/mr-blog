## WORLD

*   **Major Escalation in the Middle East:** Military hostilities between Israel and Iran have reached a critical peak, with the Israel Defense Forces launching massed strikes against Iranian targets overnight. The regional situation remains volatile, as Hezbollah has retaliated with rocket fire toward Tel Aviv, and concerns grow regarding potential disruptions to energy supplies via the Strait of Hormuz.
*   **Political Upheaval in Hungary:** Opposition party TISZA has surged in popularity, now leading Fidesz by 23 percentage points ahead of the April 12th elections. Meanwhile, Prime Minister Viktor Orbán has escalated his rhetoric, labeling Ukraine a "terrorist state" and announcing a halt to gas supplies to the country, marking a severe downturn in diplomatic relations.
*   **Diplomatic Speculation on Conflicts:** The administration of U.S. President Donald Trump has signaled confidence in a forthcoming resolution to both the Iran-Israel conflict and the war in Ukraine. Pastor Mark Burns, a spiritual advisor to the President, claims that Vladimir Putin is prepared to negotiate a settlement, though official channels have yet to confirm a breakthrough.

## POLAND

*   **High-Profile Security Operation in Warsaw:** Polish authorities, including internal security agency ABW and local police, have successfully apprehended a high-ranking organized crime figure known as a "thief-in-law" ("wor w zakonie"). The 44-year-old Ukrainian national, considered a significant security threat due to his past criminal history, was captured in a downtown hotel.
*   **Legislative Push on Housing:** The Left party (Lewica) has officially proposed a new tax targeted at residential business entities. The initiative, championed by Anita Kucharska-Dziedzic, aims to regulate the housing market and curb the influence of large-scale commercial investors on residential property availability.
