## WORLD

*   **Nuclear Diplomacy in the Middle East:** U.S. Vice President JD Vance has announced a significant breakthrough in talks with Iran, stating that Tehran has agreed to permit the return of nuclear inspectors. The U.S. government has responded by easing sanctions, signaling a potential shift in regional policy and a foundation for what officials describe as "lasting peace."
*   **Tragedy at Qatari Gas Facility:** A technical malfunction during a restart operation at a major Liquified Natural Gas (LNG) site in the Ras Laffan industrial zone has resulted in at least 13 deaths and 66 injuries. The incident comes during a volatile period in the Middle East, further stressing regional industrial security.
*   **Escalation in the Donbas:** Despite diplomatic overtures elsewhere, the war in Ukraine remains brutal. Russian forces have intensified their aerial campaign against the "fortress belt" cities of Sloviansk and Kramatorsk, triggering a mass exodus of civilians fleeing the bombardment.

## POLAND

*   **Ukrainian Strike on Russian Strategic Infrastructure:** In a significant tactical development, Ukrainian forces have launched a drone attack on a space communication center in Dubna, located near Moscow. While Russian officials claim primary systems remain operational, the strike marks a notable reach into Russian territory.
*   **Diplomatic and Political Tensions:** Controversy continues surrounding the rebuilding of Ukraine, with President Karol Nawrocki notably absent from the upcoming reconstruction conference in Gdańsk. Meanwhile, ruling party figures have signaled a desire to de-escalate recent verbal conflicts with Kyiv to focus on domestic stability.
*   **Unrest in the UK:** A police intervention in Southampton has led to the death of Henry Nowak, a student of Polish origin. The case has sparked public outrage and violent protests, resulting in the arrest of several Polish nationals who now face legal repercussions in the United Kingdom.
