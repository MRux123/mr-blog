## WORLD

* **High-Stakes Hungarian Elections:** Hungary is witnessing a historic day as citizens head to the polls in record numbers. Voters are deciding the future of Viktor Orbán’s long-standing administration, with opposition figures—most notably Péter Magyar—capitalizing on significant grassroots momentum.
* **Escalating Tensions in Eastern Europe:** Despite declarations of ceasefire agreements for the Orthodox Easter period, both Ukraine and Russia have reported widespread violations. The Ukrainian General Staff cited thousands of breaches, highlighting the fragility of any peace efforts in the ongoing conflict.
* **Middle East Diplomatic Impasse:** Hopes for a swift resolution to the conflict involving Iran have dimmed following reports that U.S. and Iranian delegations failed to reach a peace deal during marathon negotiations in Pakistan. Vice President JD Vance confirmed that American terms for ending the hostilities were rejected.

## POLAND

* **Strategic Diplomatic Pivot to Asia:** Prime Minister Donald Tusk has embarked on a significant diplomatic tour across Asia. Following a stop in Tajikistan to discuss regional security, the Prime Minister has arrived in Seoul. This visit marks a crucial step in strengthening strategic ties with South Korea and Japan, signaling a shift in Polish foreign policy focus.
* **Domestic Political Friction:** The Prime Minister's international mission has drawn sharp criticism from the domestic political opposition. Presidential advisor Błażej Poboży publicly questioned Tusk’s political strategy, drawing controversial comparisons between the current government and the leadership style of the late-PRL era.
