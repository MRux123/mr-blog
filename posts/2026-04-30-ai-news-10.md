## WORLD

*   **Hybrid Warfare and Security Concerns in Europe:** Investigations are underway across Europe regarding a series of attacks on Jewish targets, believed to be orchestrated by a shadowy Islamist group. Analysts suggest these incidents reflect an evolving strategy of "hybrid warfare," utilizing low-cost, unsophisticated tactics intended to sow fear and destabilize social cohesion.
*   **Geopolitical Standoff in the Middle East:** Israel has intercepted a Gaza-bound flotilla in international waters near Crete, detaining 175 activists. The incident highlights the ongoing friction between pro-Palestinian advocacy groups and Israeli naval enforcement, further complicating the already volatile regional climate.
*   **Trump’s Proposed Troop Cuts:** Following criticism from German Chancellor Merz regarding US involvement in Iranian conflicts, former President Trump has announced that the US is evaluating a potential withdrawal or reduction of troops stationed in Germany, signaling a possible shift in transatlantic security alliances.

## POLAND

*   **RARS Corruption Investigation:** The Internal Security Agency (ABW) has conducted further arrests related to irregularities in the Rządowa Agencja Rezerw Strategicznych (RARS). Prosecutors allege that corrupt practices involving massive coal contracts have caused a loss to the public interest estimated at over 530 million PLN.
*   **Parliamentary Clashes Over Ministerial Accountability:** The Polish Sejm has become a focal point of intense political friction, as the opposition pushes for a vote of no confidence against the Minister of Climate and Environment and the Minister of Health. Prime Minister Donald Tusk dismissed the motion as politically motivated, accusing the opposition of attempting to disrupt parliamentary proceedings rather than providing substantive arguments.
*   **Landmark Ruling on Mortgage Credits:** The Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU) has issued a significant ruling regarding Swiss franc-indexed mortgages. The court has declined to support the mass invalidation of domestic loans that were later converted into foreign currency, marking a pivotal moment for thousands of Polish borrowers currently embroiled in legal battles with financial institutions.
