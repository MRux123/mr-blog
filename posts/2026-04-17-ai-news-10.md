## WORLD

* **Iran’s Military Resilience Under Scrutiny:** Despite significant infrastructure damage and the loss of high-ranking leadership, Western intelligence assessments suggest that Iran retains critical military capabilities. This analysis, highlighted by Bloomberg, raises urgent questions about the potential for further escalation should current ceasefire efforts fail, positioning Iran as a continued wildcard in regional stability.
* **USA Extends Invitation to Iranian National Team:** In a diplomatic pivot, the United States has officially invited the Iranian national football team to participate in the 2026 World Cup. The move, confirmed by White House World Cup officials, marks a notable shift in tone for the tournament co-hosted by the U.S., signaling a move toward sports diplomacy despite ongoing geopolitical frictions.

## POLAND

* **Kaczyński Rejects Morawiecki’s Independent Initiative:** Tensions within the Law and Justice (PiS) party have intensified as Jarosław Kaczyński issued a firm ultimatum regarding former Prime Minister Mateusz Morawiecki’s new association. Kaczyński explicitly stated that individuals engaging with the new initiative will be barred from future electoral lists, highlighting a struggle for party loyalty and structure.
* **European Energy Security Concerns:** The Polish government has addressed growing anxieties over jet fuel supplies in Europe. While Deputy Minister Wojciech Wrochna emphasized that national reserves remain sufficient for over 90 days and currently require no intervention, the situation reflects broader regional concerns regarding energy infrastructure stability amidst shifting economic conditions.
* **Legislative Disruptions in the Sejm:** Parliamentary proceedings were interrupted by a significant disciplinary action as Włodzimierz Czarzasty expelled two PiS deputies from the chamber. The incident highlights the highly polarized nature of the current Polish political landscape and the increasing difficulty in maintaining decorum during legislative sessions.
