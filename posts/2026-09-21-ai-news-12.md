## WORLD

* **Post-Election Russia and the Kremlin’s Narrative:** Following the recent elections, the Kremlin is leveraging the results to frame the domestic public as staunch supporters of the ongoing military operations in Ukraine. Analysts suggest this move is designed to solidify domestic cohesion despite international scrutiny and a lack of significant electoral surprises.
* **U.S.-China Relations Strained Over Taiwan Arms:** A proposed $14 billion arms sale to Taiwan has emerged as a significant point of friction in the U.S.-China relationship. As Beijing exerts pressure on the Trump administration to halt the transaction, Taiwan is increasingly emphasizing the strategic necessity of its security for broader regional stability and U.S. interests.
* **Challenges to Europe’s Identity:** Populist movements across Europe are gaining momentum by focusing on national identity and pride. These narratives are finding resonance among voters feeling disenfranchised by globalization and rapid societal shifts, complicating the traditional political landscape of the continent.

## POLAND

* **Judicial Impasse in the Constitutional Tribunal:** A procedural conflict has escalated as Maciej Berek, newly elected to the Constitutional Tribunal, has officially petitioned the President to facilitate his swearing-in. The move highlights the ongoing gridlock regarding the appointment of judges and the lack of communication between the presidential office and key judicial figures.
* **UOKiK Penalizes Pharmaceutical Giants:** In a major antitrust development, the Office of Competition and Consumer Protection (UOKiK) has imposed nearly 1 billion PLN in fines on four major pharmaceutical wholesalers. The companies were found to have colluded on pricing and commercial conditions for over a decade, artificially inflating medication costs for Polish consumers.
