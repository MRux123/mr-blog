## WORLD

*   **Humanitarian Crisis in Ceuta:** A massive surge of approximately 60,000 migrants from Morocco into the Spanish enclave of Ceuta has triggered a severe political and humanitarian emergency. Official reports confirm at least 43 fatalities. The influx, representing nearly 70% of the city’s population, has placed immense strain on local infrastructure and ignited diplomatic friction within the European Union, with Madrid facing criticism from regional partners over its border management.
*   **Church of England Reparations:** The Archbishop of Canterbury, Dame Sarah Mullally, has reaffirmed a £100m commitment to support communities historically affected by transatlantic slavery. During her visit to Cape Coast Castle in Ghana, she expressed profound regret for the Church’s historical involvement in the slave trade, framing the fund as a symbolic gesture of repentance.

## POLAND

*   **Geopolitical Tensions Over Missile Incident:** The Polish Ministry of Foreign Affairs has summoned the Russian ambassador following the impact of a Russian Ch-101 missile in the Lublin region. Prime Minister Donald Tusk confirmed the incident, which occurred on Thursday morning, marking a significant escalation in security concerns along Poland’s eastern border.
*   **Airspace Provocation:** In a related security development, Polish F-16 fighter jets were scrambled to intercept a Russian Ił-20 reconnaissance aircraft detected approximately 40 km off the coast of Kołobrzeg. The Ministry of National Defense continues to monitor increased aerial activity in the Baltic region.
*   **Strategic Infrastructure Oversight:** The Central Port of Communication (CPK) has undergone a major leadership change, with the Supervisory Board dismissing the company’s CEO and a board member. This decision marks a pivotal shift in the governance of Poland’s most significant strategic infrastructure project.
