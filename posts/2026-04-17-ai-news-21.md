## WORLD

* **De-escalation in the Strait of Hormuz:** International tensions have slightly subsided as reports confirm that maritime traffic is resuming in the Strait of Hormuz. Following a temporary agreement between Iran and the United States, a convoy of approximately 20 vessels has been observed navigating the strategic waterway. Despite these signs of de-escalation, diplomatic sources remain cautious, noting that significant disagreements regarding nuclear policy and the potential export of enriched uranium persist between the two nations.
* **Heightened Security Concerns in Eastern Europe:** Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has issued a stern warning to Belarusian leader Alexander Lukashenko. Citing intelligence reports regarding increased military infrastructure development and artillery positioning near the border, Zelensky suggested that Russia is once again attempting to pressure Belarus into the ongoing conflict. He cautioned Minsk to learn from recent geopolitical shifts in Venezuela to avoid making a strategic error.

## POLAND

* **Presidential Stance on Crypto Legislation:** President Karol Nawrocki has defended his decision to veto the cryptocurrency market bill, stating he has "not regretted it for a second." The President shifted the blame for the Zondacrypto exchange controversy, which affected an estimated 30,000 individuals, directly onto the current administration under Donald Trump's political rival, Prime Minister Donald Tusk.
* **National Bank of Poland Ensures Asset Security:** NBP Governor Adam Glapiński has conducted an unprecedented personal inspection of Poland’s gold reserves stored at the Federal Reserve Bank in New York. The move, intended to dispel public concerns, emphasizes the security of national assets amid a complex economic and political climate.
