## WORLD

* **Strait of Hormuz Blockade**: Tensions in the Middle East have reached a critical juncture as Iran has officially closed the Strait of Hormuz. The IRGC announced that the waterway will remain inaccessible until the U.S. lifts the blockade on Iranian ports, explicitly stating that approaching ships will be treated as military targets. This move is being framed by Tehran as a retaliatory action against alleged breaches of the existing ceasefire.
* **Political Earthquake in Hungary**: In a historic shift, the TISZA party, led by Péter Magyar, has won the Hungarian national elections with a landslide, securing 141 out of 199 seats in the National Assembly. This victory grants the party a constitutional majority, effectively ending Viktor Orbán’s 16-year tenure in power.
* **U.S.-Cuba Diplomatic Push**: U.S. officials have arrived in Havana to present a formal set of proposals for economic and political reforms. The delegation emphasized that the current administration is offering only a limited window of opportunity for Cuba to implement these changes, signaling a potential new chapter in Caribbean diplomatic relations.

## POLAND

* **Domestic Media and Social Discourse**: Polish media outlets are heavily focused on the regional security fallout, with extensive coverage of the Iranian blockade and its potential impact on global energy prices. Simultaneously, public interest has shifted toward international geopolitical commentary, specifically regarding the ongoing friction between the U.S. administration and the Vatican, as well as the internal Russian crisis prompted by high-profile social media criticism directed at President Putin.
