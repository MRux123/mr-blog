## WORLD

* **Political Polarization in Spain:** As the country heads toward a pivotal election, the potential entry of the far-right Vox party—led by Santiago Abascal—into government signals a possible departure from the post-Franco political consensus. Meanwhile, economic challenges, particularly in housing, remain the focal point of the final campaign debates.
* **Ukrainian Security and Global Relations:** President Zelensky has publicly questioned the "strange" relationship between Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin, expressing concerns over the current trajectory of Western support. Simultaneously, Ukrainian intelligence warns of a growing security threat as Russia develops its own satellite network, "Rasswiet," intended to compete with Starlink.
* **Repression of Historical Memory in Russia:** Under the current administration, the Kremlin continues its systematic rehabilitation of Stalin-era policies, effectively erasing the documented horrors of the Gulag system as a means to solidify the current state ideology during the ongoing conflict.

## POLAND

* **Judicial and Political Standoff:** In a significant escalation of political tensions, the General Prosecutor has filed a formal motion to lift the immunity of former Justice Minister Zbigniew Ziobro. The request is directly linked to an ongoing investigation into the deployment of the Pegasus spyware system.
* **Conflict Over Presidential Vetoes:** President Karol Nawrocki’s recent veto of tax and sugar tax reforms has sparked intense backlash from the government. Officials from the Ministry of Health claim the veto will cause a 1.3 billion PLN deficit for the National Health Fund (NFZ), while proponents of the President’s decision argue it protects small businesses and consumers from excessive financial burdens.
* **Economic Diplomacy:** President Nawrocki’s visit to the Sierra Gorda mine in Chile has been framed as a diplomatic success, highlighting Poland's international footprint in critical resource sectors and strengthening industrial ties.
