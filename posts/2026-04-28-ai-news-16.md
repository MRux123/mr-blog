## WORLD

* **UAE Announces Withdrawal from OPEC:** In a historic shift for the global energy market, the United Arab Emirates has officially declared its intention to exit the OPEC oil cartel. After nearly six decades of membership, the UAE's move is widely interpreted as a potential existential threat to the organization's influence, particularly as the country has frequently voiced dissatisfaction with rigid production quotas.
* **Resurgence of Somali Piracy:** International maritime security is under pressure as three vessels have been hijacked off the Horn of Africa within the last week. Analysts suggest that the recent surge in piracy is a direct consequence of international naval assets being redirected to other conflict zones, leaving critical shipping lanes vulnerable to criminal exploitation.

## POLAND

* **High-Profile Prisoner Exchange:** In a major diplomatic development, Polish journalist and activist Andrzej Poczobut has been released from detention in Belarus. Prime Minister Donald Tusk confirmed the exchange, which included a clergyman and a Belarusian citizen, sparking national debate regarding the strategic trade-offs made to secure the release.
* **Political Fallout Over Diplomatic Strategy:** The release of Poczobut has triggered domestic political friction. While the government celebrates the return of an "European hero," critics—led by Karol Nawrocki—have sharply condemned Prime Minister Tusk’s recent comments to the *Financial Times* concerning the exchange, characterizing the rhetoric as irresponsible and detrimental to Poland’s long-term national interests.
