## WORLD

* **Escalating Conflict in Iran:** Tensions in the Middle East have reached a critical juncture, with U.S. forces conducting direct strikes against Iranian targets to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. While U.S. officials maintain a firm stance against a cease-fire, stating the U.S. is "obliterating the other side," Iran’s leadership, including Modżtaba Chamenei, has issued defiant messages claiming the "enemy has been defeated." Analysts warn that the region is being pushed toward a full-scale land invasion, potentially destabilizing the global economy through the disruption of energy flows.
* **Geopolitical Pressure on Energy Security:** The ongoing conflict is exacerbating energy supply concerns, as evidenced by the Ukraine-Hungary pipeline dispute. The suspension of oil flows through a key hub in Western Ukraine, damaged by Russian strikes, threatens both energy supplies and critical EU financial mechanisms, further complicating the European security landscape.
* **Sanctions Against Hezbollah Networks:** The U.S. Department of the Treasury has expanded its sanctions reach into Poland, targeting an individual and a Warsaw-based firm accused of money laundering for Hezbollah, signaling a wider international effort to choke the financial lifelines of Iranian-backed militant groups.

## POLAND

* **PSL Challenges Presidential "SAFE 0%" Legislation:** The Polish People's Party (PSL) has announced plans to introduce a revised version of the presidential "SAFE 0%" bill to the Sejm next week. Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of National Defence Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz stated that the new proposal will address shortcomings in the original draft by President Karol Nawrocki, specifically aiming to ensure that support reaches a broader range of state services while utilizing funds from the National Bank of Poland (NBP).
