## WORLD

*   **Ebola Crisis Escalates in Central Africa:** The Democratic Republic of Congo and Uganda are facing a worsening Ebola outbreak, with health authorities reporting over 130 suspected deaths and 513 cases since mid-May. The World Health Organization warns the situation could persist for months, drawing criticism from U.S. officials, including Secretary of State Marco Rubio, regarding the speed of the international response amidst domestic public health budget cuts.
*   **Strategic Pivot in Russia-China Relations:** As international attention remains fixed on the Persian Gulf conflict, Vladimir Putin has traveled to Beijing to deepen energy and strategic ties with Xi Jinping. The summit is marked by notable diplomatic signals, including the rare use of Chinese license plates on the Russian presidential limousine, highlighting Russia’s increased reliance on Beijing as it seeks to offset economic pressures.

## POLAND

*   **U.S. Military Presence in Europe Under Scrutiny:** Tensions surrounding the rotational deployment of U.S. troops in Poland and Germany continue to dominate political discourse. While U.S. Vice President J.D. Vance characterized the pause in rotations as a delay rather than a permanent reduction or punitive measure, EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas warned that this reflects a broader American strategic shift toward the Indo-Pacific, forcing Europe to confront its changing security architecture.
*   **Polish Delegation Detained in Middle East:** A diplomatic incident has unfolded involving the "Global Flotilla Sumud," as Israeli forces detained a group of Polish citizens. Reports from the organization indicate that the intervention included the use of live fire toward a vessel, marking a significant escalation and creating a complex consular challenge for Polish authorities.
