## WORLD

*   **Geopolitical Friction Over U.S.-Russia Diesel Deal:** International relations remain strained as European officials express deep concern regarding a new energy agreement between the United States and Russia. The deal, described by Ukraine as a "gift" to President Putin, has forced European leaders to adopt a cautious diplomatic stance to manage the resulting security implications.
*   **Escalating Maritime Conflict in the Black Sea:** Tensions in the Black Sea have intensified following a drone attack that reportedly sank a merchant ship off the coast of Bulgaria. The Bulgarian government has summoned the Russian ambassador, suspecting Moscow’s direct involvement in the strike, further complicating regional trade and security stability.
*   **Political Stagnation in Israel:** Despite the seismic shift in the political landscape following the October 7 attacks, internal discourse in Israel remains polarized. Ahead of upcoming elections, the electorate is deeply divided between blaming Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu for security negligence and criticizing the military establishment for alleged failure, stalling efforts for unified political progress.

## POLAND

*   **Regional Security Alerts Amidst Ukrainian Conflict:** The Government Security Centre (RCB) issued an urgent alert for the Lublin Voivodeship following a ballistic missile attack on Kyiv. Polish military aviation was mobilized to monitor the airspace, reflecting the persistent threat posed by the ongoing war across the eastern border.
*   **Government Crackdown on Anti-State Threats:** Polish authorities have reported a series of arrests linked to online threats directed at state officials. Vice-Premier Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz acknowledged the rising frequency of these incidents, marking a significant domestic security challenge for the current administration.
*   **Proposed Legislative Changes Regarding Migrant Assistance:** The Ministry of Internal Affairs and Administration has proposed controversial amendments to the Penal Code that could criminalize humanitarian aid provided to undocumented migrants. Legal experts warn that the broad language of the proposed law might inadvertently penalize acts of basic assistance, sparking a heated national debate on ethics and the rule of law.
