## WORLD

* **Escalation in Iran and Food Security Risks**: The ongoing conflict involving Iran is beginning to disrupt global supply chains, specifically regarding fertilizer production. Industry leaders warn that these shortages threaten crop yields worldwide, potentially triggering a significant rise in food prices. Simultaneously, internal political friction within the Iranian leadership suggests instability, as top officials dispute the conduct of nuclear negotiations with the United States.
* **Russian Sabotage Plots in Europe**: Lithuania has successfully dismantled a series of Russian-backed sabotage and murder plots. This operation highlights the persistent security threats facing Europe, particularly as Western diplomatic focus has increasingly shifted toward crisis management in the Middle East.
* **Uganda’s Legislative Crackdown on Dissent**: The Ugandan government is accelerating a restrictive "protection of sovereignty" bill that could impose up to 20 years in prison for activities labeled as promoting "foreign interests." International observers view this as a strategic effort to suppress political opposition and civil society, drawing parallels to autocratic tactics observed in Russia and China.

## POLAND

* **22 Years in the European Union**: Poland marks the 22nd anniversary of its accession to the European Union. While Prime Minister Donald Tusk emphasized the historic significance and success of the move, the anniversary has underscored a deep political divide, with opposition figures like Zbigniew Sasin questioning the nature and impact of the country's integration.
* **Constitutional Debate Stalls**: The proposal for a presidential council focused on drafting a new constitution has been rejected by the Left. Vice Premier Krzysztof Gawkowski stated that his party would not participate, citing a lack of respect for the existing constitution by the President and calling for adherence to democratic values rather than structural changes.
* **Political Accountability**: In a minor but notable domestic incident, Senator Mieczysław Golba (PiS) faced legal consequences after being caught speeding, resulting in the loss of his driving license. Notably, the senator declined to invoke his parliamentary immunity, an action that deviates from typical high-profile political responses to traffic infractions in the country.
