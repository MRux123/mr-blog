## WORLD

* **Imminent De-escalation in the Persian Gulf:** After months of low-intensity conflict and regional uncertainty, diplomatic efforts have reached a critical juncture. Reports suggest a memorandum between the United States and Iran, aimed at ending hostilities, may be signed as early as Sunday in Geneva. While both parties remain cautious, the potential agreement includes the lifting of oil sanctions and the unfreezing of state funds, signaling a major shift toward regional stability.
* **Tensions Rise in Northern Ireland:** Belfast is currently on edge following two consecutive nights of violent, anti-immigrant rioting. Local authorities have confirmed that the unrest, sparked by a recent stabbing incident, has evolved into targeted attacks based on the victims' skin color, raising significant concerns regarding social cohesion and public safety in the region.

## POLAND

* **EU Approves KPO Amendments:** The European Union has officially accepted adjustments to Poland’s National Recovery Plan (KPO). A pivotal change is the removal of the requirement to implement fees for internal combustion engine vehicles. These funds have been redirected toward strategic investments in heating infrastructure and satellite internet, paving the way for the next payment request from Brussels.
* **Historical Discovery in Huta Pieniacka:** Archaeologists and researchers have uncovered a mass grave in Huta Pieniacka in western Ukraine. Initial findings by the Institute of National Remembrance (IPN) suggest the site contains the partially burned remains of Polish victims murdered during the 1940s, marking a significant development in the ongoing efforts to document historical atrocities in the region.
* **Controversy Over Student Rights Legislation:** The Polish educational system faces internal friction as the "Act on the Rights and Obligations of Students" moves through the legislative process. While the Ministry of Education argues the law will standardize school procedures, teachers' unions and pedagogical experts warn that the introduction of a formal rights-advocacy system could overly bureaucratize the school environment, shifting it away from a community-based educational model.
