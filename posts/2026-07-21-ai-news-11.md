## WORLD

*   **Escalation in the Red Sea:** The Houthi militia in Yemen has announced a blockade of the Red Sea directed at Saudi Arabia, raising significant concerns about the potential for a direct expansion of the ongoing U.S.-Iran conflict. This move threatens international shipping lanes and signals a further deterioration of stability in the Middle East.
*   **Political Shifts in Nicaragua:** President Daniel Ortega has explicitly declared the end of competitive elections in Nicaragua. The move formalizes the country's trajectory toward an entrenched autocracy, drawing widespread condemnation and reinforcing existing sanctions from Western powers.
*   **Lebanese Buffer Zone Deployment:** Following a U.S.-brokered agreement, the Lebanese army has initiated a deployment into a designated "pilot zone" following a partial Israeli troop withdrawal, marking a fragile but potentially significant step toward de-escalating the border conflict.

## POLAND

*   **Public Healthcare Reform:** Prime Minister Donald Tusk has outlined plans to cap the earnings of medical professionals in the public healthcare sector, suggesting a limit of 16 times the minimum wage (approximately 76,800 PLN monthly). The government plans to initiate the legislative process this September, fueling public debate over fiscal discipline in medicine.
*   **Russian Mobilization Discrepancies:** While official Russian reports claim 141,000 recruits were brought into the military during the spring mobilization, independent observers such as the Conflict Intelligence Team (CIT) have cast doubt on these figures, suggesting a significant mismatch between state narrative and reality.
