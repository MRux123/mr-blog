## WORLD

*   **Truce in Oleshky:** Following intense diplomatic mediation involving the White House, Ukraine and Russia have agreed to a temporary ceasefire in the occupied city of Oleshky. The move, aimed at facilitating urgent humanitarian aid for residents living in dire conditions, has raised hopes for broader trilateral negotiations between Ukraine, the U.S., and Russia.
*   **Shifting U.S. Policy on Ukraine:** Reports from *The Economist* indicate that a potential U.S. peace plan could involve Ukraine securing membership in the European Union in exchange for renouncing long-term NATO aspirations. While the proposal aims to de-escalate the conflict, the willingness of the Kremlin to engage remains uncertain.
*   **Rising Tensions in the West Bank:** The annual olive harvest has been marred by an unprecedented spike in violence, with Israeli settlers launching attacks on Palestinian farmers and besieging homes. International concern is mounting, with U.S. officials joining the growing chorus of condemnation regarding the escalating insecurity in the occupied territory.

## POLAND

*   **Diplomatic Response to Church Attack:** The Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MSZ) has officially addressed the recent attack on a church, confirming that a Polish-Jordanian family was among those affected. The incident has drawn significant attention to the safety of Polish citizens abroad and the broader security climate.
*   **Government Resignations and Political Accountability:** The political landscape remains fluid following the rapid resignation of officials. Former deputy minister Jan Szyszko has openly criticized the concept of "apolitical" government positions, highlighting the tensions within current administrative reforms and the fragile nature of political mandates within the coalition.
