## WORLD

* **Escalation in Iran-U.S. Tensions:** The standoff in the Strait of Hormuz has intensified, with Tehran accusing the United States of "reckless" military actions. While diplomatic channels remain open regarding a potential peace proposal, global markets are already reacting to the conflict, with energy, banking, and defense sectors reporting significant financial gains amidst the instability.
* **Victory Day Ceasefire Violations:** Despite a declared truce intended to coincide with Victory Day commemorations, both Russian and Ukrainian forces have reported renewed hostilities, including hundreds of drone strikes. The breakdown of the ceasefire underscores the persistent difficulty in establishing stable de-escalation zones.
* **UK Political Landscape Shifts:** Preliminary results from the United Kingdom’s local elections indicate substantial losses for Prime Minister Keir Starmer’s Labour Party. While Starmer has accepted responsibility for the municipal setbacks, he has dismissed calls for his resignation, signaling a period of political turbulence for the administration.

## POLAND

* **Controversy Over the SAFE Program:** The Polish government’s signing of the SAFE financial agreement has sparked a fierce political divide. While the ruling coalition defends the program as a vital step for national security, opposition leaders and the Presidential Chancellery have voiced strong concerns regarding long-term national debt and the implications of foreign-currency borrowing on Polish sovereignty.
* **Proactive Defense Assessments:** Amidst heightened regional security concerns, experts have released a sobering report outlining potential economic costs—ranging from 0.8 to 5.5 trillion PLN—associated with various scenarios of an external attack on Poland. The report aims to quantify the nation's strategic and financial exposure in the event of an escalation.
* **Probing the Puszcza Solska Fire:** The Zamość District Prosecutor’s Office has launched an official investigation into the recent fire at Puszcza Solska. Authorities are actively debunking circulating disinformation and conspiracy theories that suggested foreign sabotage, emphasizing that there is currently no evidence of arson.
