## WORLD

*   **Political Instability in New Zealand and Israel:** Following a tightly contested general election, New Zealand faces weeks of complex coalition negotiations due to a deeply divided parliament. Meanwhile, in Israel, Gadi Eisenkot is positioning himself to challenge Benjamin Netanyahu’s premiership, though the path to forming a new government remains fraught with systemic obstacles.
*   **Rising European Security Concerns:** A wave of hybrid threats is sweeping across Europe, with authorities reporting a surge in cyberattacks—including incidents targeting critical infrastructure like supermarket refrigeration systems—alongside persistent bomb threats against aviation. Governments are increasingly emphasizing public resilience and preparedness.
*   **Shifting US Political Landscape:** The incoming US administration’s appointment of Stephen Miller as Chief of Staff signals a confrontational approach to governance, setting the stage for significant clashes between the White House and Congress.

## POLAND

*   **Tragedy in Krakow:** The nation is in mourning following a mass shooting in the heart of Krakow. An attacker, operating from a vehicle near the Main Market Square, killed six people and left 12 others injured. The assailant, a Polish citizen, is currently in police custody, and authorities have dismissed reports of a second shooter.
*   **Official Response and Investigation:** Prime Minister Donald Tusk is traveling to Krakow to oversee an emergency crisis management meeting. President Andrzej Duda and international leaders, including Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelensky, have condemned the attack. Law enforcement has discovered significant quantities of additional weaponry and ammunition in the perpetrator's vehicle, and a full investigation into the motive and scope of the attack is underway.
