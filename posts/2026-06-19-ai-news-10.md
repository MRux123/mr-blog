## WORLD

* **Mideast Instability:** The recently signed U.S.-Iran agreement, intended to stabilize the region, faces a severe test as violence in Lebanon escalates. Clashes between Israeli forces and Hezbollah have resulted in at least 18 deaths in Lebanon and four Israeli soldiers killed, contradicting the truce terms. Consequently, Switzerland has postponed a critical round of U.S.-Iran talks, casting doubt on the long-term viability of the diplomatic breakthrough.
* **Democratic Contraction in Zimbabwe:** Zimbabwe’s parliament has passed a controversial bill to extend President Emmerson Mnangagwa’s term by two years, while simultaneously abolishing direct presidential elections. This legislative maneuver marks a significant shift in the country's governance, drawing international attention to the weakening of democratic processes in the region.

## POLAND

* **Legal Accountability for Zbigniew Ziobro:** The Polish Sejm has voted to lift the immunity of former Justice Minister Zbigniew Ziobro. The motion, filed by National Prosecutor Dariusz Korneluk, pertains to private prosecution regarding statements made by Ziobro during a press conference last year, signaling a major development in the ongoing scrutiny of the former administration.
* **Presidential Legislative Activity:** President Karol Nawrocki continues to consolidate his legislative legacy, announcing the signing of six new bills into law. With 229 acts signed since the start of his term and 37 vetoes exercised, the President remains a central figure in shaping the current Polish legal landscape amidst ongoing political debates.
* **Security Concerns in Poznań:** Authorities have launched an investigation into the discovery of GPS tracking devices attached to a freight train wagon in Poznań. The incident, which has been reported to the Internal Security Agency (ABW), raises serious questions regarding potential espionage or illegal surveillance operations targeting critical transport infrastructure.
