## WORLD

* **Middle East Ceasefire Agreed:** Following prolonged tensions, Israel and Hezbollah have reached an agreement for a ceasefire in Lebanon. While the move is intended to facilitate stability, concerns remain regarding the impact on broader regional diplomatic efforts, specifically those concerning the ongoing conflict between the United States and Iran.
* **Global Reparatory Justice Framework:** International leaders meeting in Ghana have adopted a new 18-point strategic roadmap for reparatory justice. This landmark agreement, emerging from a significant UN-backed forum, outlines measures to address historical legacies of enslavement and economic debt burdens, marking a major milestone in international human rights diplomacy.
* **Shift in U.S. Funding Policy:** The United States government has announced a phased withdrawal of HIV prevention and treatment funding in South Africa. The decision, reportedly linked to concerns regarding the protection of the Afrikaner minority, signals a potential disruption in long-standing health cooperation between the two nations.

## POLAND

* **Diplomatic Crisis over Order of the White Eagle:** President Karol Nawrocki has revoked the Order of the White Eagle, Poland's highest state decoration, previously awarded to Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky. The decision was prompted by the naming of a Ukrainian military unit after figures associated with the UPA. The move has drawn sharp criticism from the Ukrainian Foreign Ministry, with Minister Andrij Sybiha labeling it a "strategic mistake" and announcing his intent to return his own Polish state decoration in protest.
* **Ultimatum to Belarus:** In a hardening of regional security rhetoric, President Zelensky has issued a one-week deadline to Alexander Lukashenko, demanding the removal of transmission equipment from Belarusian territory that facilitates Russian drone strikes against Ukraine. Kyiv has signaled it will take unilateral action if the demand is not met.
* **Severe Weather Impact:** Southern and Western Poland are grappling with the aftermath of violent storms that caused widespread infrastructure damage. Emergency services reported hundreds of interventions, including the flooding of medical facilities and significant damage to local transport, highlighting the strain on emergency response systems during extreme weather events.
