## WORLD

*   **Hezbollah Rejects U.S.-Brokered Ceasefire:** Tensions in the Middle East have escalated following Hezbollah's explicit rejection of a ceasefire agreement between Israel and Lebanon. Despite the U.S. announcing a breakthrough on Wednesday night, the Iran-backed group confirmed it was not a party to the negotiations, leading to a continuation of hostilities and further regional instability.
*   **Growing Concerns Over NATO's Eastern Flank:** The security landscape in Europe remains volatile as concerns mount regarding potential future threats from Russia. Coupled with Ireland’s move to bolster its military capabilities to address perceived neutrality gaps, the international community is closely monitoring the technological arms race, specifically regarding the mass production of military drones.
*   **Controversy Over Ebola Response in Kenya:** The U.S. administration is facing significant criticism from public health experts and officials over plans to establish an American-only Ebola quarantine center in Kenya. Despite a local court block, the U.S. government has proceeded with the deployment of responders, sparking an ethical and diplomatic debate regarding health protocols and sovereignty.

## POLAND

*   **European Commission Targets Poland with Legal Action:** The Polish government faces significant financial risks as the European Commission has initiated legal proceedings against the country. The lawsuit, filed before the Court of Justice of the EU, centers on the failure to implement essential regulations concerning the emissions trading system (ETS) within the aviation sector.
*   **Political Mobilization Ahead of June 4th:** Prime Minister Donald Tusk has announced a "Great March of Patriots" scheduled for June 4, 2027. The event, which will take place in Warsaw, is symbolically tied to the planned construction of a monument honoring Tadeusz Mazowiecki, the first Prime Minister of the Third Polish Republic, outside the Chancellery.
*   **Security Services Under Scrutiny:** The National Security Bureau (BBN) is currently defending its actions following an internal controversy regarding the publication of a photograph showing the uncovered faces of special forces operators. While BBN maintains that the release was authorized by event organizers, the incident has triggered an intense national debate over the operational security of Poland’s elite military units.
