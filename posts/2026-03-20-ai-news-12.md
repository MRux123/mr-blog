## WORLD

* **Escalating Conflict in the Middle East:** The war involving Iran continues to dominate global headlines. Ongoing drone attacks on oil infrastructure, including a major Kuwaiti refinery, have kept oil markets volatile. Meanwhile, analysis suggests a growing strategic rift between the U.S. and Israel regarding regional long-term objectives, as Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu signals that military action alone may not achieve the desired political outcome in Tehran.
* **Geopolitical Tensions over Ukraine:** The Hungarian government is facing significant diplomatic pressure as it threatens to veto vital Ukraine loans. Prime Minister Viktor Orbán has conditioned the release of funds on the repair of a Russian oil pipeline crossing Ukraine, a stance that European allies have characterized as disloyalty and blackmail amidst the ongoing regional crisis.
* **Leadership Crisis in Madagascar:** The nation’s military-led government has introduced a controversial policy requiring cabinet ministers to pass lie detector tests. This mandate follows a series of abrupt dismissals and reflects the ongoing instability and corruption concerns following the recent coup led by Michael Randrianirina.

## POLAND

* **Resolution of Officer Promotion Impasse:** A significant breakthrough in civil-military relations has occurred as President Karol Nawrocki agreed to the appointment of 78 new officers. This decision, made in consultation with the Minister of National Defence, Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz, effectively ends a period of tension regarding military staffing.
* **Legal Dispute Over Same-Sex Marriage:** The ruling Law and Justice (PiS) party has announced an immediate challenge to the Supreme Administrative Court (NSA) following its recent ruling. The court suggested that constitutional provisions regarding marriage do not strictly forbid the recognition of same-sex unions contracted elsewhere in the EU, prompting fears from the opposition regarding future legislative changes.
* **Technological Advancement in Defense:** Poland is set to commence mass production of an advanced anti-drone system dubbed the "Monster from Tarnów." The government has allocated approximately one billion PLN from the "SAFE" program to accelerate the domestic manufacturing of this technology, which has already garnered international interest.
