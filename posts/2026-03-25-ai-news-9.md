## WORLD

* **Escalation in the Baltic Region:** Security concerns in Northern Europe have intensified following reports that Russian drones violated the airspace of Latvia and Estonia. In a provocative incident, a drone struck a power plant in Auvere, Estonia. While local authorities confirmed no disruption to the energy grid, the simultaneous nature of these incursions marks a significant escalation in regional instability.
* **Middle East Standoff:** The path to a ceasefire in the Middle East remains obstructed after Iran unveiled a set of stringent demands, including reparations and control over the Strait of Hormuz. International observers and U.S. officials have dismissed these conditions as unrealistic, further dampening hopes for an immediate diplomatic resolution, even as global markets react to U.S. mediation efforts.
* **Humanitarian Tragedy in Kenya:** Authorities have uncovered a mass grave containing dozens of bodies, including 25 children, in Kenya. The discovery has prompted an investigation into local hospitals and morgues, as the nature of these deaths remains under scrutiny.

## POLAND

* **Rise of Foreign Organized Crime:** Minister Tomasz Siemoniak addressed growing public concern regarding the rise of brutal, Eastern European criminal syndicates operating within Poland. Official data from the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBŚP) indicates that a significant majority of foreign nationals apprehended for gang activities are Russian-speaking, prompting the government to emphasize the need for enhanced police efficacy.
* **Industrial Tragedy:** Poland mourns the loss of a 42-year-old miner following a fatal accident at the ROW-Jankowice coal mine. The incident occurred 700 meters underground, leading to an official expression of condolences from President Karol Nawrocki, as the mining community once again faces the harsh realities of workplace safety in the sector.
