## WORLD

*   **Heightened Tensions in the Middle East:** Following the reported seizure of vessels near Iran, global markets are reacting sharply, with oil prices climbing above $100 per barrel. The situation remains volatile as the White House signals it does not currently view these seizures as a violation of existing cease-fire agreements, even as the region remains under the shadow of a new collective leadership structure in Iran.
*   **Massive EU Aid Package for Ukraine:** In a significant shift of strategy following a 99% decline in U.S. aid, the European Union has pledged a $106 billion loan to Ukraine. Unlike previous packages, this allocation is heavily focused on military spending, signaling a long-term commitment to the conflict.
*   **Political Uncertainty in Bulgaria:** The election of Rumen Radev, known for his pro-Russian stance, has sparked concern among EU partners. The international community is closely monitoring his first moves as leader to see whether he will align Bulgaria with Brussels or strengthen ties with Moscow.

## POLAND

*   **Legal Action Against MEPs:** The European Parliament's legal committee has officially moved to revoke the immunity of four Polish Members of the European Parliament. This high-profile development could have significant repercussions for the political landscape in Warsaw.
*   **TSUE Ruling on Consumer Credit:** The Court of Justice of the European Union (TSUE) has issued a landmark ruling regarding consumer loans. The decision mandates that banks must impose stricter limits on interest rates for consumer credit and installment loans, a move expected to provide relief to many borrowers.
*   **Financial Controversies in Kraków:** The city of Kraków is navigating a fiscal hurdle regarding its upcoming local referendum. With the cost of the vote estimated at 4 million PLN, officials have determined that funds will be sourced from VAT refunds to ensure the process proceeds on May 24th.
