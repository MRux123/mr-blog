## WORLD

*   **Security Breach in Damascus:** French President Emmanuel Macron narrowly avoided a significant security threat during his visit to Syria, as explosions were reported near his hotel. While French officials confirmed the President remains unharmed and his official itinerary will proceed, the incident underscores the volatile security environment in the region.
*   **Marine Le Pen’s Legal Crossroads:** France faces a potential political earthquake as a court prepares to deliver a verdict on Marine Le Pen’s embezzlement conviction. Should the court uphold the conviction, the leader of the National Rally—who currently leads polls for the 2027 presidential race—could be formally barred from candidacy, triggering a major restructuring of the French political landscape.
*   **NATO’s Strategic Reconfiguration:** As world leaders convene in Turkey, the Atlantic alliance is grappling with the necessity of a new operational model. With pressure mounting from the United States to shift more defense responsibility toward Europe, the summit represents a critical juncture for NATO's long-term cohesion and relevance in an evolving global security architecture.

## POLAND

*   **Diplomatic Standoff with Ukraine:** Tensions between Warsaw and Kyiv have reached a new high as Polish officials confirmed that no formal meeting between President Karol Nawrocki and President Volodymyr Zelensky will take place during the NATO summit in Ankara. The dispute, exacerbated by recent comments from the Minister of National Defense regarding military aid, signals a significant cooling in bilateral relations.
*   **Investigative Breakthrough in Monaco Attack:** Ukrainian authorities have discovered the body of a primary suspect linked to an assassination attempt on a Ukrainian oligarch in Monaco. The discovery near Kyiv has taken a dark turn, with reports indicating that an active military intelligence officer and a former police official have been detained in connection with the woman's death.
