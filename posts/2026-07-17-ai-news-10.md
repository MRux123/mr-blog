## WORLD

*   **Escalation in the Middle East:** U.S. Central Command has launched a series of targeted strikes against Iranian military infrastructure, including bridges and port facilities in the country's south. The operation, described as targeting military assets, marks a significant intensification of regional tensions.
*   **Political Shifts in the United Kingdom:** Andy Burnham, the prominent politician known as the "King of the North," is set to be confirmed as the new leader of the Labour Party this Friday. His elevation signals a potential shift in British governance as he prepares to assume the role of prime minister.
*   **Ukraine's Defense Reform:** A leadership transition is underway within the Ukrainian government, as the young administration, which successfully streamlined weapon procurement processes, faces pressure from established political factions. The departure of the defense minister highlights the ongoing friction between the "old guard" and the new generation of policymakers.

## POLAND

*   **Institutional Turmoil and Leadership Changes:** The Polish political landscape remains volatile following the election of Mateusz Szpytma as the new head of the Institute of National Remembrance (IPN). His appointment, intended to succeed Karol Nawrocki—who now holds the office of President of Poland—awaits final confirmation by the Senate.
*   **Executive Conflict:** A significant rift has emerged between Prime Minister Donald Tusk and President Karol Nawrocki. The tension stems from the President's recent veto of the "next of kin" legislation, which the government had positioned as a crucial societal compromise. Tusk has publicly condemned the move as a breach of political boundaries.
*   **Legislative Progress on Internet Safety:** In a rare point of consensus, Minister of Digital Affairs Krzysztof Gawkowski welcomed the signing of legislation banning "patostreaming," signaling a move toward stricter regulation of digital content and online safety in Poland.
