## WORLD

* **Controversy over D-Day commemorations:** US Secretary of Defence Hegseth has sparked a diplomatic rift by criticizing Europe's handling of migration during his speech in Normandy. Marking 82 years since the liberation of north-western Europe, his remarks have drawn attention to the shifting priorities in transatlantic relations regarding border security and migration policy.
* **Escalating tensions in Armenia:** On the eve of national elections, authorities have detained six candidates from a pro-Russian opposition party. The move, executed without official justification, has raised significant concerns regarding the integrity of the democratic process and the country's political stability.
* **Papal solidarity in Spain:** Pope Francis has commenced a seven-day diplomatic tour of Spain, using the platform to praise the nation’s commitment to peace and its humanitarian stance toward migrants, contrasting sharply with rising nationalist rhetoric in other parts of the continent.

## POLAND

* **High-level diplomatic tensions:** The unexpected visit of Kyrylo Budanov to Warsaw has ignited a heated political debate among Polish leadership. Former Prime Minister Leszek Miller characterized the visit as a provocation, while others, including Bronisław Komorowski, interpret the move as a necessary diplomatic maneuver to test the potential for future cooperation.
* **Public safety concerns:** A massive search operation is currently underway in Poznań for a missing 10-year-old girl named Amelia, who was last seen at a local playground. Authorities are urging the public to assist in the investigation as the situation remains critical.
* **Tragic incidents across the country:** Local communities are reeling from several fatal accidents, including the death of a three-year-old child following a drowning incident in Małopolska and a serious car-train collision in Bulowice involving a 69-year-old driver. These events have prompted renewed calls for improved safety protocols in both domestic and public spaces.
