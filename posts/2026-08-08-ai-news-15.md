## WORLD

*   **U.S. Diplomatic Retrenchment:** The State Department has notified Congress of plans to close five consulates across Canada, Japan, Indonesia, Cameroon, and Grenada. The move, framed as a cost-cutting measure under the Trump administration's foreign policy review, has sparked concerns among critics regarding a potential diplomatic vacuum that may be leveraged by China.
*   **Strait of Hormuz Standoff:** Hopes for the reopening of the vital Strait of Hormuz have been dampened following a statement from Iran’s Supreme National Security Council. Tehran has issued a list of specific conditions that must be met by the United States before the waterway can be reopened, signaling a prolonged period of regional volatility.
*   **Trump Administration Appointments:** The U.S. Senate has narrowly confirmed Todd Blanche, Donald Trump’s former criminal defense lawyer, as the new Attorney General. The confirmation follows a rare internal pushback within the Republican party, marking a significant consolidation of power within the Department of Justice.

## POLAND

*   **Escalating Rhetoric with Russia:** The Polish Ministry of Foreign Affairs has issued a firm response to the latest series of threats from Russian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Maria Zakharova. This exchange, which also involved rebuttals from Deputy Prime Minister Radosław Sikorski, highlights the currently deteriorating diplomatic climate between Warsaw and Moscow.
*   **Hate Crime Investigation in Krakow:** Police have detained a 39-year-old man who turned himself in after a public verbal attack against a Ukrainian couple in Krakow. Authorities are treating the incident as a nationality-based insult, a charge that carries a potential sentence of up to three years in prison, reflecting Poland's ongoing efforts to address rising social tensions.
