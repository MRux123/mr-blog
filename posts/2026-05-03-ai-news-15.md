## WORLD

*   **US-China Standoff Over Iranian Oil:** The trade landscape faces increased volatility as China formally rejects US sanctions imposed on five of its oil refineries. Beijing maintains that the sanctions, linked to Iranian oil trade, are illegitimate and violate international law, setting the stage for a broader economic standoff between the two superpowers.
*   **Missing US Personnel in Morocco:** A search and rescue mission is currently underway led by the US Africa Command to locate two US service members reported missing in Morocco. Details surrounding the circumstances of their disappearance remain limited as operations continue.
*   **Wartime Ordinance Explosion in Austria:** A tragic incident in Upper Austria saw five children injured—some seriously—after a remnant from a past conflict exploded under their campfire. The event serves as a stark reminder of the lingering dangers posed by unexploded ordnance in European territories.

## POLAND

*   **Clarification on Defense Procurement:** Minister of National Defence Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz has moved to soothe public concerns regarding Patriot missile battery deliveries. While confirming that there are currently no signals of delays for the air defense systems, he acknowledged that ongoing conflicts in the Middle East may impact the timeline for other US-supplied military hardware.
*   **Constitutional Debate:** Amidst Third of May celebrations, the debate regarding potential constitutional amendments has intensified. Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz argued that the state's focus should remain on the maturity and responsibility of its political leadership rather than systemic legislative overhauls, a sentiment that has prompted varied responses from coalition partners, including Prime Minister Donald Tusk.
