## WORLD

* **Apple Announces Leadership Transition**: Technology giant Apple has initiated a historic succession plan. After 15 years at the helm, Tim Cook will transition to the role of executive chairman in September, with John Ternus set to take over as the company's new CEO. This move signals a significant strategic pivot for one of the world's most influential corporations.
* **Japan’s Security and Geological Challenges**: Japan is navigating a period of heightened instability. Following a major seismic event, the meteorological agency remains on alert for potential further earthquakes. Simultaneously, the government has formally moved to loosen arms export restrictions, marking a significant departure from the nation's post-WW2 pacifist doctrine and allowing for expanded defense cooperation with international partners.
* **Geopolitical Uncertainty in US-Iran Relations**: Tensions surrounding the Middle East remain volatile as the Trump administration attempts to orchestrate a peace deal. Mixed signals from Tehran and Washington, coupled with the announcement that Vice President JD Vance will lead a delegation to Pakistan, highlight the fragile nature of current diplomatic maneuvers aimed at cooling regional conflicts.

## POLAND

* **Political Realignment within the Right-Wing Opposition**: Recent internal dynamics within the Law and Justice (PiS) party suggest an ongoing struggle for identity. High-profile meetings between Jarosław Kaczyński and Mateusz Morawiecki have fueled speculation regarding the party's future direction, while critics like Adrian Zandberg suggest the party is struggling to retain its voter base by shifting further toward the radical right.
* **Emergency Contraception Policy Update**: The Ministry of Health has announced a continuation of the pilot program regarding the "day after" pill. The initiative, which allows pharmacists to issue emergency contraception prescriptions to patients aged 15 and older, will be extended beyond its originally scheduled conclusion in June, ensuring continued access to reproductive health services.
