## WORLD

* **Trilateral Diplomacy on Ukraine:** In a significant shift in global power dynamics, US President Donald Trump, Russian President Vladimir Putin, and Chinese leader Xi Jinping held trilateral talks regarding the war in Ukraine. Trump characterized the dialogue as a "two-against-one" scenario, noting that he and the Chinese leader coordinated efforts to push for a resolution, though he admitted that achieving lasting peace is proving more difficult than initially anticipated.
* **Middle East Security Update:** Iraqi security forces have entered a stronghold of the Iran-backed militia, Kataib Hezbollah, for the first time in over a decade. This move marks a potential turning point in internal Iraqi stability, although the long-term impact on the militia’s presence and regional influence remains uncertain.
* **UK-EU Post-Deal Relations:** Following the recent agreement on trade, food exports, and youth exchange schemes, UK officials—specifically Burnham—have expressed a strong desire to explore even closer ties with the European Union, signaling a potential thaw in post-Brexit relations.

## POLAND

* **Security Incidents at the Border:** Polish authorities have reported multiple "Russian objects" entering Polish airspace during joint military exercises by Ukraine's allies. While officials confirmed no physical damage or injuries occurred, the government has officially characterized these incursions as a provocative act by Moscow. Military analysts suggest this reflects the ongoing pressure on NATO’s eastern flank.
* **Strategic Security Debate:** Amidst the border tensions, high-ranking military officials, including General Mieczysław Bieniek, have addressed public calls for NATO to "close the sky" over Ukraine. General Bieniek cautioned that such an action would effectively place NATO in a state of war with Russia, emphasizing that any defense strategy must be coordinated through a unified alliance framework rather than unilateral escalation.
* **Internal Judicial Reforms:** In a move to assert accountability within the legal system, Minister Waldemar Żurek has suspended a deputy prosecutor amid an investigation into oversight within the organized crime and corruption division, reinforcing the administration's stance that no official is above legal scrutiny.
