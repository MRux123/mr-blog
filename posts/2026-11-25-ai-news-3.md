## WORLD

*   **Huawei Convicted of Trade Secret Theft:** A New York court has found Huawei guilty of stealing trade secrets in a landmark trial. While the company was acquitted of racketeering charges, the verdict marks a significant legal setback for the tech giant, potentially impacting its international operations and regulatory standing.
*   **Escalating Violence in Riyadh:** Tensions in the Middle East have surged following a Houthi strike on a skyscraper in the Saudi capital, Riyadh. The attack left 11 people wounded, marking a dangerous escalation in the ongoing conflict between the Iran-backed militia and Saudi authorities.
*   **Legal Scrutiny of French Leadership:** Former French President Nicolas Sarkozy and his wife, Carla Bruni, are set to stand trial. The proceedings will address serious allegations of witness tampering related to historical investigations into the illegal financing of past election campaigns, further complicating the French political landscape.

## POLAND

*   **High-Level Security Consultations:** In light of the recent violation of Polish airspace by Russian assets, government officials are engaged in critical diplomatic discussions in Washington. The talks aim to coordinate a robust response and reinforce regional security architecture.
*   **Diplomatic Protest Against IAEA Appointment:** The Polish government joins Ukraine in expressing sharp disapproval regarding the appointment of Aleksei Ferapontov as Deputy Director General of the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA). The move has prompted a formal diplomatic protest, citing concerns over the neutrality and security implications of the selection.
*   **Church Stance on Controversial Burial:** The Sandomierz Curia has issued a formal statement regarding the burial of the perpetrator behind the recent Krakow shooting. The Diocese clarified that the responsibility for organizing the funeral lies with the authorized family members, following earlier reports of a local parish priest’s refusal to conduct the rite.
