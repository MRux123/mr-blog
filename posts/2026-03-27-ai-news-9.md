## WORLD

*   **Heightened Tensions in the Strait of Hormuz:** Global energy security remains precarious as President Trump issues an ultimatum to Iran, threatening attacks on power infrastructure unless the vital oil shipping lane is fully reopened by April 6. The situation is further destabilized by ongoing military strikes initiated by Israel.
*   **Symbolic Change in US Currency:** In an unprecedented move marking a shift in executive visibility, the US Treasury has confirmed that President Donald Trump’s signature will appear on new paper currency, a development currently drawing significant international attention.
*   **Leadership Transition in Nepal:** Former rapper Balendra Shah has officially assumed office as the Prime Minister of Nepal. His landslide victory reflects widespread public frustration with systemic corruption, positioning him as a reformist figure tasked with stabilizing the nation.

## POLAND

*   **Legislative Amnesty for Foreign Legionnaires:** President Karol Nawrocki has signed a package of five new laws, most notably an amnesty provision for Polish citizens who fought in Ukraine. This legislation effectively clears the legal record for those involved in the conflict since 2014, signaling a shift in the state's approach to veterans of the Ukrainian front.
*   **Reforming Driving Standards:** The Ministry of Infrastructure has announced significant plans to overhaul the Polish driving examination process, specifically targeting the removal of the traditional maneuvering yard test. The proposal has sparked a polarized national debate regarding road safety and administrative modernization.
*   **Infrastructure Challenges in Łódź:** Minister Dariusz Klimczak has publicly addressed the critical status of the Łódź railway tunnel project, which requires the demolition of at least four residential buildings. The government continues to characterize the project's current operational state as a legacy of systemic failures inherited from the previous administration.
