## WORLD

*   **Escalating Conflict in Odessa and Tupase:** The war in Ukraine has intensified, with Russian forces launching strikes on residential infrastructure and port facilities in Odessa, resulting in casualties. In a retaliatory measure, Ukrainian forces successfully conducted their fourth strike on the port of Tupase, marking a significant escalation in the ongoing regional conflict.
*   **Fertilizer Crisis Looming:** Yara International, the world’s largest fertilizer producer, has warned that the ongoing conflict involving Iran could trigger a global supply crisis. The CEO cautioned that competition for limited resources could lead to food shortages and skyrocketing prices, disproportionately affecting vulnerable communities across Africa.
*   **Venice Biennale Controversy:** The prestigious Venice Biennale is facing an internal crisis as the jury resigns just days before the exhibition's opening. The mass departure follows rising tensions surrounding the decision to allow Russia’s participation for the first time since the full-scale invasion of Ukraine.

## POLAND

*   **Constitutional Reform Initiative:** The President has announced the formation of a council tasked with drafting a new constitution. The body, which will include representatives from various political factions and expert groups, is expected to present a proposal before the end of the presidential term in 2030, sparking significant debate regarding the country's legislative future.
*   **Labor Law Amendments:** Starting May 1, new regulations grant employees in the private sector the right to include civil-law contracts (*umowy-zlecenia*) in their total service record. This change, which follows similar implementations in the public sector, requires the Social Insurance Institution (ZUS) to issue relevant certifications, aimed at improving labor protections for workers.
*   **Presidential Veto on Divorce Reform:** The President has officially vetoed legislation concerning out-of-court divorce procedures. Officials characterized the proposed model as a "Las Vegas-style divorce," arguing that such simplified legal dissolutions of marriage would be detrimental to national demographics and family policy.
