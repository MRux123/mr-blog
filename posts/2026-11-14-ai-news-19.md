## WORLD

*   **High-Stakes Geopolitical Summit:** Kremlin officials have confirmed that U.S. President Donald Trump, Russian President Vladimir Putin, and Chinese leader Xi Jinping are scheduled to meet on November 19 in Shenzhen. While the gathering signifies a rare moment of trilateral dialogue, the White House has clarified that no private bilateral meeting between the U.S. and Russian presidents is planned, underscoring the delicate diplomatic nature of the encounter.
*   **Tragedy in Egypt:** A major rail disaster in the al-Ayyat district, south of Cairo, has left at least seven people dead and over 125 injured. The incident, involving a collision between two passenger trains, has prompted an immediate investigation by the Egyptian government into the cause of the derailment.
*   **Election Postponement in South Sudan:** The nation’s first-ever national election, originally slated for December, has been officially delayed. Analysts had expressed significant concerns regarding the legitimacy of the vote, marking a continued period of political uncertainty for the country since its independence in 2011.

## POLAND

*   **Diplomatic Leadership Ambitions:** Poland is positioning itself as a central player in regional security, joining ongoing negotiations concerning the Russian invasion of Ukraine. Czech Prime Minister Andrej Babisz has publicly expressed support for Warsaw to take a lead role in representing Central and Eastern European interests, as the region grapples with shifting European security architectures.
*   **Political Restructuring:** Polska 2050 has officially scheduled elections for a new party leader for December 2. Following the resignation of previous leadership, the party is transitioning into a new phase, with figures like Paweł Śliz confirming their candidacy and clear requirements set for potential nominees.
*   **AfD’s Unconventional Strategy:** In a move that has drawn international attention, the German political party AfD has nominated former football star Thomas Berthold as their candidate for the German presidency. While the move aims to capture public interest, mainstream analysts view Ilse Aigner (CSU) as the front-runner for the office.
