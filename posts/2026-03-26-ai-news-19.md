## WORLD

* **Iran Partially Relaxes Strait of Hormuz Blockade:** Following global economic pressure caused by the restriction of oil shipments, Iran has granted passage through the Strait of Hormuz to specific nations, including China, India, and Russia. Despite this move, the United States continues to exert significant pressure on Tehran, with reports suggesting that Israeli forces have neutralized a key Iranian naval commander responsible for the blockade operations.
* **IOC Implements Gender Eligibility Rules:** The International Olympic Committee has finalized a major policy shift, announcing that the women's category in future Olympic Games will be restricted exclusively to biological females starting in 2028. This decision marks a significant milestone in international sporting regulations under the current IOC leadership.
* **Political Turmoil in Hungary:** As the country approaches critical elections, the government of Viktor Orbán faces mounting international scrutiny. Allegations of mass voter intimidation, bribery, and the initiation of espionage cases against investigative journalists have drawn sharp criticism from observers regarding the state of democratic processes in Hungary.

## POLAND

* **Government Moves to Reduce Fuel Prices:** The Polish cabinet has officially adopted draft legislation aimed at lowering fuel prices, primarily through adjustments to VAT and excise taxes. Prime Minister Donald Tusk emphasized that while the move is intended to protect household budgets, it will be executed within the framework of responsible fiscal policy.
* **Jan Urban Sets Strategy for Albania Match:** In the sports sector, national team manager Jan Urban has finalized the squad for the upcoming encounter with Albania, drawing significant interest from the Polish football community as the team prepares for the next phase of competition.
* **Local Infrastructure Crisis in Grodziec:** A significant social issue has emerged in the Silesian village of Grodziec, where residents face potential eviction following the shutdown of residential buildings due to illegal sewage management systems. The situation highlights a complex legal failure dating back to the privatization of property by a state-run institute.
