## WORLD

*   **New Paradigm in Espionage:** Intelligence agencies in the Netherlands have identified an alarming trend where foreign actors are shifting away from traditional professional spies. Instead, they are increasingly utilizing online platforms to recruit civilians for acts of sabotage and information gathering—a "crime-as-a-service" model that has gained significant momentum following the invasion of Ukraine.
*   **Escalation at the Belarusian Border:** The Latvian intelligence service has issued a stern warning regarding Belarus's evolving tactics in the migrant crisis. Officials report that Belarusian authorities are now providing formal combat training to migrants, using propaganda and indoctrination to prepare them for direct confrontations with European border guards.
*   **Infrastructure Paralyzed in Germany:** A massive strike at Berlin-Brandenburg Airport has resulted in the cancellation of 445 flights. Approximately 57,000 passengers are affected as 2,000 workers demand improved wages and better working conditions, highlighting growing labor tensions across the European transport sector.

## POLAND

*   **Billion-Zloty Energy Strategy:** Prime Minister Donald Tusk has announced a monumental investment plan, earmarking one trillion zlotys for the energy sector over the next decade. Citing the volatile geopolitical climate, Tusk emphasized the need for energy sovereignty and has initiated diplomatic efforts within the EU to secure continued industrial emission allowances to protect Polish manufacturing.
*   **Security Concerns Regarding RBN:** The Internal Security Agency (ABW) has officially notified the prosecutor's office concerning the participation of Sławomir Cenckiewicz in the National Security Council (RBN), signaling a potential legal and political probe into the vetting or legitimacy of high-level advisory appointments.
*   **Diplomatic Intervention Over Stolen Artifact:** The Polish Embassy in Kyiv has launched an urgent intervention after an item identified as the Virtuti Militari order—awarded to an officer murdered by the NKVD in 1940—appeared for sale on a Ukrainian online marketplace. Authorities are urging the public to refrain from participating in the auction while they investigate the origin of the item.
