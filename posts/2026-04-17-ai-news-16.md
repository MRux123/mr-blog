## WORLD

* **Strait of Hormuz Reopens, Triggering Oil Market Plunge:** Global energy markets have reacted sharply to Iran’s announcement that the Strait of Hormuz will remain open for commercial shipping for the duration of the current ceasefire. Brent crude prices dropped by approximately 10%, reaching their lowest point in five weeks, providing much-needed relief to global supply chains.
* **Tensions in the Middle East De-escalate:** Following the ceasefire between Israel and Lebanon, U.S. President Donald Trump publicly claimed credit for preventing further Israeli bombardment, stating he has effectively prohibited such actions. However, the situation remains fragile, as the U.S. maintains a strict blockade policy toward Iran, creating a complex and volatile diplomatic landscape.

## POLAND

* **Coalition Stability Under Pressure:** The Polish government is facing a significant internal crisis. Minister Katarzyna Pełczyńska-Nałęcz has publicly rejected Prime Minister Donald Tusk’s ultimatum regarding the vote of no confidence for Climate Minister Paulina Hennig-Kloska. The disagreement over the leadership's "ultimatum style" of governance is being described by coalition members as a potential path to political instability.
* **Polling Shifts and Political Realignment:** Recent CBOS polling reveals a record high for the Civic Coalition (KO) at 32%, marking their best result since April 2025. Conversely, the Law and Justice party (PiS) has seen a historical decline in support to 18.2%. Despite these numbers, the long-term viability of a stable government remains a subject of intense debate.
* **Protest Climate Shifts:** The activist group "Ostatnie Pokolenie" (Last Generation) has officially announced the end of their operations in Poland. After two years of high-profile protests that frequently disrupted urban traffic, the group claims their mission is complete, signaling a notable cooling of climate-related social unrest in the country.
