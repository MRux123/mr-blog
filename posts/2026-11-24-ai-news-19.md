## WORLD

*   **Japan Challenges U.S. Stance on International Criminal Court:** In a significant diplomatic divergence, Japanese leader Sanae Takaichi has publicly defended the International Criminal Court (ICC). Takaichi has pledged to accelerate financial support for the body, directly countering the Trump administration's ongoing efforts to weaken the international institution. This move signals a potential rift in the U.S.-Japan alliance regarding global judicial oversight.
*   **Nvidia Investigation Heightens Regulatory Pressure:** U.S. Attorney General inquiries into Nvidia have intensified, leading to a 7% decline in the company’s share price. Lawmakers are demanding transparency regarding the reinstatement of CEO Jensen Huang, specifically questioning the potential influence exerted by the Trump administration in the process. The situation underscores the growing volatility surrounding AI-driven markets and political interference.
*   **Escalating Humanitarian Crisis in Syria:** Reports confirm a sharp rise in land mine casualties in Syria, two years after the primary conflict. With nearly 1,000 children among the victims of unexploded ordnance, international observers are calling for more robust demining efforts to prevent further civilian loss.

## POLAND

*   **Legal Controversy Over ECHR Ruling:** Jarosław Kaczyński has harshly criticized a recent ruling by the European Court of Human Rights (ECHR) concerning the situation on the Polish-Belarusian border. The former Prime Minister characterized the decision as a manifestation of the "madness" of European institutions, further inflaming the ongoing debate regarding the intersection of national sovereignty and international human rights law.
*   **Proactive Stance on Security Threats:** In the wake of discussions regarding Russian missile trajectories, Deputy Minister of National Defence Paweł Zalewski has issued a firm statement. Addressing concerns over whether Polish defense systems can reliably predict the impact points of incoming Russian Ch-101 cruise missiles, Zalewski emphasized that the state’s security policy is grounded in strategic readiness rather than reliance on chance.
*   **High-Level Legal Scrutiny of Former Official:** The investigation into the Zondacrypto case has taken a serious turn, with a former Central Anti-Corruption Bureau (CBA) agent, Artur Chodziński, facing formal charges. Justice Minister Waldemar Żurek confirmed the development, noting that the prosecution has requested a three-month arrest, signaling an expanding scope in this complex financial and political investigation.
