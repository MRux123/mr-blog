## WORLD

* **Middle East Instability:** The ceasefire in the Middle East is rapidly unraveling following a fresh round of retaliatory strikes between the U.S. and Iran. The conflict, centered on disputes over the Strait of Hormuz, has entered a volatile phase, with Iran’s military increasingly assertive. This situation follows the death of the former Emir of Qatar, Sheikh Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani, marking the end of an era for the gas-rich nation.
* **Loss of a Key U.S. Strategist:** Republican Senator Lindsey Graham, a central figure in U.S. foreign policy and an outspoken proponent of a hawkish stance on Iran, has passed away. His death leaves a significant void in the American political landscape, particularly regarding the future of U.S. engagement in the Middle East and ongoing support for Israel.

## POLAND

* **Tensions in Polish-Ukrainian Relations:** Polish officials and academics are engaging in a candid debate regarding the historical and current complexities of Polish-Ukrainian relations. Following statements by Polish diplomats concerning Ukrainian victims of the Polish state, public discourse has shifted toward reconciling the support for Ukraine's war effort with the necessary acknowledgment of historical grievances.
* **Security and Foreign Policy Stance:** Foreign Minister Radosław Sikorski has issued a stern response to recent threats from the Kremlin, emphasizing that while Poland is no longer acting out of panic, it remains vigilant. Warsaw is adopting a firm stance, suggesting that any further Russian aggression would result in significant strategic costs for Moscow.
* **Legislative and Diplomatic Deadlocks:** Poland continues to push for tougher EU sanctions against Russia, though negotiations have hit a stalemate. The pressure on European ambassadors to reach a consensus is mounting as the strategic window for effective economic deterrence begins to narrow.
