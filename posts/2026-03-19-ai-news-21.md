## WORLD

* **Middle East Conflict Update:** Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has declared that Iran’s capacity for uranium enrichment and ballistic missile production has been neutralized following a 20-day sustained air campaign by U.S. and Israeli forces. Meanwhile, global markets remain volatile as the Trump administration scrambles to reassure the American public, suggesting potential measures to lower oil prices, including the paradoxical possibility of lifting sanctions on specific Iranian oil exports.
* **Historical Accountability:** In a significant legal development, a Brussels court has ruled that a 93-year-old former Belgian diplomat, Étienne Davignon, must stand trial for his alleged complicity in the 1961 assassination of Patrice Lumumba, the first prime minister of the independent Congo. This trial is viewed by observers as a long-awaited reckoning with the colonial past of Belgium.

## POLAND

* **Diplomatic Pressure on Belarus:** The Polish government continues to push for the release of journalist Andrzej Poczobut, who remains imprisoned by the Lukashenko regime. Interior Minister Marcin Kierwiński stated that, despite the complexity of the situation and the recent release of 235 other individuals by Minsk, Poland remains committed to continuous diplomatic and intelligence efforts to secure Poczobut's freedom.
* **Energy Policy Gridlock:** Warsaw is currently engaged in a high-stakes debate within the European Union regarding the future of the Emissions Trading System (ETS). As rising energy costs burden member states, Poland is leading a coalition of nations demanding significant structural reforms to the system, setting the stage for a contentious upcoming European Council summit.
