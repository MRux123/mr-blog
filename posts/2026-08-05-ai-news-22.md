## WORLD

* **U.S. Strategic Retrenchment:** The U.S. State Department has formally notified Congress of its intent to shutter five foreign consulates across Asia, Africa, and North America. The move, framed as a cost-cutting measure under the current administration, has sparked concerns among geopolitical analysts regarding a potential "diplomatic vacuum" that could be exploited by China to expand its influence in these regions.
* **Middle East Maritime Tensions:** Security in the Gulf remains precarious as Iran announces an imminent shipping route agreement with Oman concerning the Strait of Hormuz. Concurrently, Houthi militants have intensified their rhetoric, threatening to expand Red Sea attacks and claiming strikes on Saudi oil tankers, further destabilizing regional energy security.

## POLAND

* **Extradition Uncertainty:** The status of former Justice Minister Zbigniew Ziobro remains a point of contention in Polish politics. Prosecutor General Waldemar Żurek has confirmed that Polish authorities have received no feedback from U.S. officials regarding ongoing requests for Ziobro's return to Poland to face legal proceedings, leaving the process effectively stalled.
* **Judicial Controversies:** Public frustration with the judiciary has been reignited following a recent court decision to deny an arrest warrant for a repeat offender caught driving with 2.5 promille of alcohol in his system, despite the individual having eight active driving bans. The ruling has prompted renewed debates over the efficacy of the Polish legal system in addressing serial traffic offenses.
