## WORLD

* **France Approves Assisted Dying Legislation:** The French parliament has passed a landmark bill allowing assisted dying for terminally ill adults. The legislation, which follows years of intense debate, establishes strict criteria to regulate the practice, marking a significant shift in the nation’s social and medical policies.
* **Global Debt Crisis in Developing Nations:** A new report from UNESCO reveals that 113 developing countries currently spend more on servicing foreign debt than on education. The findings highlight a systemic economic issue, with some nations allocating up to five times more resources to loan repayments than to the fundamental development of their youth, exacerbated by a projected 30% decline in global education aid.
* **Geopolitical Sentiment Shift:** A recent Pew study indicates a notable shift in international perceptions, with an increasing number of countries expressing greater confidence in China’s leadership under Xi Jinping compared to the United States under Donald Trump. 

## POLAND

* **Political Turmoil within PiS:** Internal friction continues to define the landscape of the Law and Justice (PiS) party. While official spokespeople have attempted to downplay disagreements regarding future assistance for Ukraine, high-profile figures have publicly criticized the political standing of Przemysław Czarnek. The party leadership has also imposed strict bans on members participating in independent political organizations, sparking defensive reactions from figures like Mateusz Morawiecki.
* **Leadership Transition Discussions in Ukraine:** President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has officially proposed Serhij Korecki as the candidate for the position of Prime Minister of Ukraine. The announcement, made alongside European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, signals a potential broader reorganization of the Ukrainian government structure.
