## WORLD

*   **Heightened Tensions in Iran-US Conflict:** The ongoing U.S. blockade of Iranian ports continues to dominate global headlines. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth reaffirmed the administration's commitment to the policy, noting that 34 vessels have been intercepted since the directive was issued. Iran has explicitly linked the resumption of diplomatic talks to the immediate lifting of these maritime restrictions.
*   **NATO Unity Under Pressure:** Reports of internal Pentagon discussions regarding the potential suspension of member states—specifically Spain and the UK—due to perceived lack of support for the conflict in Iran have sparked alarm. While NATO officials maintain there is no legal provision for expulsion, the discourse highlights a growing rift within the alliance regarding collective military obligations and regional security strategies.

## POLAND

*   **Escalating Rhetoric from the Kremlin:** Relations between Warsaw and Moscow have reached a new low following provocative comments from a senior Kremlin envoy, Kirill Dmitriev. In response to Prime Minister Donald Tusk’s remarks regarding the absence of Russian officials from a recent EU meeting, Dmitriev issued a veiled threat involving future energy dependency.
*   **Political Accountability and Scandals:** Domestic politics remain volatile as calls for the resignation of PKOI head Radosław Piesiewicz intensify, supported by statements from various sports federations. Concurrently, Prime Minister Tusk has publicly condemned the Zondacrypto controversy, citing alleged ties between the firm and Russian organized crime, labeling the case as suspicious from its inception.
*   **Legal Developments:** The political landscape is further complicated by the decision of the Regional Prosecutor's Office in Warsaw to partially dismiss the investigation into the alleged "coup d'état" attempt, specifically concerning pressure exerted on the National Electoral Commission (PKW) regarding the financial reporting of the Law and Justice (PiS) party.
