## WORLD

* **Escalation in the Middle East:** Tensions have reached a critical juncture as NATO forces intercepted and shot down an Iranian ballistic missile that violated Turkish airspace. Meanwhile, the U.S. continues to signal potential direct military action against Iranian energy infrastructure, specifically threatening the key export terminal on Kharg Island. These developments have forced regional shifts, with Spain denying the U.S. access to military bases for operations related to the conflict.
* **UN Slavery Resolution:** In a historic move, the United Nations has officially classified the transatlantic slave trade as the "gravest crime against humanity." This landmark ruling is expected to significantly energize the African Union’s campaign for long-term reparations, despite lingering resistance from several Western nations historically linked to the trade.

## POLAND

* **Military Financing Dilemma:** A new CBOS poll highlights a divided public regarding the funding of national defense. 51% of respondents support utilizing EU SAFE program loans for armament purchases, while 46% favor President Karol Nawrocki’s proposal to finance military acquisitions through National Bank of Poland (NBP) profits.
* **Geopolitical Security Concerns:** Amid the volatile situation in the Middle East, there is growing public and political discourse in Poland regarding the safety of Polish military assets abroad. This follows reports of the withdrawal of major U.S. naval assets from the region and the increasing frequency of missile incidents involving NATO members.
