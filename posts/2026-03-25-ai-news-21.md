## WORLD

* **UN Recognition of the Slave Trade:** In a landmark resolution, the United Nations has officially classified the transatlantic chattel slave trade as the "gravest crime against humanity." Backed by the African Union and Caricom, the motion calls for reparatory justice, marking a significant step toward addressing historical grievances at the highest international level.
* **Escalating Middle East Tensions:** Regional stability faces a critical test as reports emerge of an Israeli directive to dismantle Iranian defense industries within a 48-hour window. This follows internal concerns within the Netanyahu government regarding a proposed 15-point U.S.-brokered peace plan, which officials fear does not sufficiently curtail Iran's military capabilities.
* **Social Media Liability:** A U.S. court has delivered a verdict finding Meta and YouTube liable in a landmark social media addiction trial. The $3 million award to a plaintiff could set a dangerous precedent for tech giants, potentially opening the door for hundreds of similar litigation cases across the United States.

## POLAND

* **Economic Concerns Over Fuel Prices:** Market analysts are warning of a possible surge in fuel costs, with projections suggesting diesel prices could reach double digits. Experts attribute these potential hikes to volatile conditions in the Middle East, signaling significant inflationary pressure on Polish households in the near term.
* **Russian Export Crisis:** Moscow is facing severe economic strain as coordinated drone strikes and infrastructure damage have paralyzed approximately 40% of its oil export capacity. With global crude prices exceeding $100 per barrel, the Kremlin faces a tightening economic grip during a period of intense geopolitical isolation.
* **Public Sentiment on EU Leadership:** A new IBRIS poll indicates a complex relationship between Polish voters and the European Union. While a significant portion of the electorate expressed dissatisfaction with the performance of European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, support for Poland's continued membership in the European Union remains robust.
