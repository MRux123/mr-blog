## WORLD

*   **Israeli President Condemns Settler Violence:** In a significant shift in rhetoric, President Isaac Herzog has delivered a sharp indictment of settler attacks in the West Bank and the reported abuse of prisoners. While the role of the Israeli presidency is largely ceremonial, Herzog's intervention underscores deepening internal concerns regarding the conduct of security forces and settler groups.
*   **Ebola Outbreak Response Scrutinized:** Public health officials in the Congo are facing intense criticism as the response to a lingering Ebola outbreak finally gathers pace. Despite months of viral spread, authorities have been accused of moving too slowly, highlighting systemic gaps in regional crisis management.
*   **Ukraine Grapples with Controversial History:** The reburial of Andriy Melnyk, a 20th-century figure viewed simultaneously as an anti-Soviet resistance hero and a Nazi collaborator, has sparked intense debate. The granting of state honors for the ceremony near Kyiv reflects the country's ongoing struggle to reconcile its complex history amidst the current war.

## POLAND

*   **Kraków Leadership Toppled by Referendum:** In a historic political shock, residents of Kraków have voted overwhelmingly to oust both Mayor Aleksander Miszalski and the City Council. Late poll results indicate that roughly 97% of participants supported the removal of the mayor, signaling a total collapse of public trust in the current local administration.
*   **Opposition Capitalizes on Kraków Result:** Following the referendum, Law and Justice (PiS) leader Jarosław Kaczyński framed the result as a wider indictment of the current government's democratic credentials. He described the outcome as a "red card" for the ruling coalition, signaling an energized opposition strategy ahead of further potential clashes.
*   **Coalition Tensions Over Retirement Age:** Internal stability within the governing coalition faces new pressure as debates intensify over the equalization of the retirement age. The proposal remains a sensitive political fault line, with stakeholders questioning whether the move could trigger significant friction among coalition partners.
