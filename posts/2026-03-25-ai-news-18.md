## WORLD

* **Escalation in Iran-Israel Conflict:** Tensions in the Middle East have reached a critical juncture as Tehran dismisses recent cease-fire proposals while signaling a potential, albeit cautious, openness to further talks. Meanwhile, reports suggest Israel intends to intensify its military operations, fearing a premature end to the war before it can effectively dismantle Iran’s strategic weapons capabilities.
* **UN Resolution on Slavery:** In a historic move, the United Nations has officially recognized slavery as the "gravest crime against humanity." This landmark resolution includes a call for formal apologies and the establishment of a reparations framework, marking a significant shift in international human rights discourse.
* **Tech Giants Held Liable for Social Media Addiction:** Following a landmark five-week trial, a court has found Meta and Google liable for the addictive design of their social media platforms, a verdict that may set a global legal precedent for how technology companies are held accountable for their impact on user mental health.

## POLAND

* **Political Maneuvering within the Right-Wing Opposition:** A significant shift in the European Parliament has seen Konfederacja politician Ewa Zajączkowska-Hernik join the "Patriots for Europe" group. This move has sparked internal dialogue, with party leader Sławomir Mentzen addressing the transition, while Zajączkowska-Hernik maintains her commitment to her domestic political base.
* **Opposition Pushes for Cabinet Shake-up:** PiS and Konfederacja are coordinating a joint effort to challenge the position of the Minister of Climate and Environment, Paulina Hennig-Kloska. The opposition is reportedly seeking support from specific coalition partners to force a vote of no confidence, reflecting deepening parliamentary frictions.
* **Historical Shift in the Anglican Church:** Sarah Mullally has become the first woman to hold the office of Archbishop of Canterbury. Her inaugural sermon served as a sharp critique of institutional failures regarding abuse victims, signaling a new era of accountability for the church.
