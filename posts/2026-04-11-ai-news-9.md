## WORLD

* **High-Stakes Diplomacy in South Asia:** U.S. Vice President JD Vance has arrived in Pakistan for critical peace negotiations with Iran. As Tehran struggles with the economic fallout of prolonged conflict and significant infrastructure destruction caused by recent bombardment, the international community remains cautiously optimistic about the possibility of a long-term cease-fire.
* **Geopolitical Escalation involving China:** U.S. intelligence reports suggest that Beijing is preparing to supply Iran with new air defense systems, a move Washington has characterized as provocative. While China denies these allegations, the potential transfer of military technology adds a layer of complexity to ongoing efforts to stabilize the region.
* **Hungarian Political Shift:** As the country approaches a pivotal vote, the opposition movement led by Péter Magyar is gaining significant momentum. Large-scale demonstrations in Budapest signal a potential turning point for Viktor Orbán’s long-standing leadership, as the electorate voices growing dissatisfaction with the status quo.

## POLAND

* **Tusk Targets Opposition:** Prime Minister Donald Tusk has launched a sharp rhetorical attack against the Law and Justice (PiS) party, drawing parallels between the policies of Jarosław Kaczyński and the destabilization tactics attributed to Vladimir Putin. During a Civic Coalition council meeting, Tusk argued that the primary threat to national security remains the erosion of Polish-Ukrainian solidarity.
* **Archdiocese Transparency:** In a move toward accountability, Metropolitan Archbishop Konrad Krajewski has confirmed the continuation of an independent historical commission tasked with investigating sexual abuse cases within the Łódź Archdiocese. The commission, composed of diverse experts, will cover the period from 1945 to 2025, marking a significant effort in addressing systemic internal issues.
* **Strategic Setback on Chagos Islands:** The British government has paused the ratification of an agreement to transfer the Chagos Archipelago to Mauritius. This decision follows intense pressure from U.S. President Donald Trump, who raised concerns regarding the strategic military importance of the islands.
